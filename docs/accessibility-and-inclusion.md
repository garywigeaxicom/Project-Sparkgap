# Accessibility and Inclusion Requirements

A trustworthy AI design must account for people who cannot use the default interface, language, connectivity, or interaction pattern.

## Requirements

- Provide keyboard-accessible workflows and compatibility with assistive technologies.
- Make uncertainty, sources, refusals, and human escalation understandable without relying on color alone.
- Support low-bandwidth and text-only access where the use case permits.
- Measure quality across relevant languages, dialects, domains, and user groups.
- Provide a non-AI or human-assisted alternative for consequential workflows.
- Avoid using protected or sensitive attributes unless their use is justified, governed, and documented.
- Include affected communities and domain experts in research, evaluation, and incident review.
- Document where the system performs poorly and avoid implying universal capability.

Accessibility and inclusion findings should be treated as release evidence, not as optional product polish.
